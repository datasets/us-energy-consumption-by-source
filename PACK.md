**New dataset: US primary energy consumption by source, 1635-2025**

The US burned less coal in 2025 than in 1905. EIA puts 2025 coal consumption at 8.67 quadrillion Btu, below its estimate of 10.00 for 1905 and 38% of the 2005 peak of 22.80. In 2024 it was 7.91, the lowest since the annual series began in 1949.

Coal had only taken over from wood in the 1880s: EIA's estimates have wood ahead in 1880 and coal ahead by 1885.

One CSV joins EIA's historical estimates (selected years, 1635-1945) with its annual series (1949-2025), with each row marked by the EIA table it comes from. Public domain. {link}

title: US energy consumption by source, 1635-2025, from EIA: coal use in 2025 (8.67 quadrillion Btu) was below EIA's estimate for 1905 (10.00)

I work on DataHub. This joins two EIA series into one CSV: its estimates of US primary energy consumption for selected years 1635-1945 (every 10 years to 1845, then every 5) and its annual Monthly Energy Review figures for 1949-2025. Nothing is interpolated, there are no figures for 1946-1948, and each row says which EIA table it comes from.

Some numbers: in EIA's estimates wood is ahead of coal in 1880 (2.85 against 2.05 quadrillion Btu) and coal is ahead by 1885 (2.84 against 2.68). Coal peaked at 22.80 in 2005. In 2025 it was 8.67, below the 10.00 estimated for 1905; in 2024 it was 7.91, and the last estimate lower than that is 6.84 for 1900.

Caveats: pre-1949 coal is apparent consumption (production plus imports minus exports) from Schurr and Netschert's Energy in the American Economy, 1850-1975, so it is not strictly comparable with the modern series. To 1945 wood means fuelwood only; from 1949 it includes wood-derived fuels. Hydro, wind and solar are counted at 3,412 Btu per kWh, EIA's captured energy approach, which is lower than older fossil-fuel-equivalent figures. 2025 may be revised. Happy to answer questions on method.
